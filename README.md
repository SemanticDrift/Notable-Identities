## Overview

The notable identities of elementary algebra, the *fórmulas notables* or *productos notables* of the Spanish-language curriculum, are taught, set aside once they become routine, and met again later under other names. This paper places each identity at the step it carries in a set of constructions: the quadratic node sequence and its odd intervals, the Geometric Matrix, the Smith chart, rational rotations with Pythagorean entries, the Fibonacci and Tribonacci recurrences, and the inscribed hexagon. Every step of the algebra is shown.

The paper does not present new identities or new theorems. Every identity is classical and attributed to its source. The contribution is the map: each step can be checked against a named classical identity, and the map identifies the identities on which the exact computations depend.

---

## Key Placements

- **Euclid's Book II:** The square of a sum, the difference of squares and Legendre's identities are propositions II.4 to II.10. The difference of squares (II.5) carries the node intervals `(2n+1)r₀`, the Geometric Matrix boundaries `ad + (c/2)² = b²` and the Smith chart ratio.
- **Cubes on triangular nodes:** `n³ = T(n)² − T(n−1)²`, the arrangement of Nicomachus.
- **Pythagorean nodes:** Nodes where the odd interval is a square lie at `n = 4T(ℓ)` and give the triples of Pythagoras's rule.
- **Rotations:** The two-square identity of Brahmagupta and Fibonacci composes rotations in the plane, and Euler's four-square identity composes them in space, where the quadruple `(3, 4, 12, 13)` closes the space diagonal over the 3-4-5 triangle.
- **Gaussian and hexagonal arithmetic:** Sophie Germain's identity is the difference of squares taken twice at `e^(iπ/4)`. The difference of cubes and Argand's identity split the vertices of the inscribed hexagon (Euclid IV.15).
- **Recurrences:** The Fibonacci equation is the reciprocal of Euclid's cut in extreme and mean ratio (II.11, VI.30). Consecutive Fibonacci numbers give the rotation triples `(F(n−1)F(n+2), 2F(n)F(n+1), F(2n+1))`.

## One Relation, Several Names

Each identity is placed beside its classical source and the names it takes later in algebra, engineering and physics: the conjugate, the complex modulus and its norm, Viète's relations, rational points on the unit circle, the parallelogram law, and power balance on a transmission line. Norm relations in the split-complex numbers, the Gaussian integers, the Eisenstein integers, the integers of ℚ(√5) and the quaternions carry most of the steps. Polynomial expansions carry the relations among powers of conjugate roots.

No construction step evaluates a trigonometric function. The computations use exact rational arithmetic, exact algebraic numbers where they arise, and rational convergents for real angles.
